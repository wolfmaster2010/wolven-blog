Textured Seed Stitch Washcloth

A free knitting pattern from Wolven Fiber Co. — knit by hand on needles

Some patterns earn their keep. This washcloth is one of them: a sturdy, nubby seed stitch that scrubs well, a garter border that keeps it lying flat in the drawer, and nothing fancy standing between you and a finished cloth. It's the cloth I reach for in my own kitchen, and it's a kind first project if you're newer to knitting — the whole texture comes from one simple rule, which I'll give you below.

Finished size

Approximately 8" × 8" (20 × 20 cm)

Materials

  • Worsted weight (#4) 100% cotton yarn — I knit these in Loops & Threads Classic Cotton
  • Approximately 55–65 yards (50–60 m)
  • US size 7 (4.5 mm) needles

Gauge

18 stitches × 32 rows = 4" in seed stitch. Gauge isn't critical for a washcloth, but this gets you close to 8" square.

Abbreviations

  • k = knit
  • p = purl
  • st(s) = stitch(es)
  • RS = right side · WS = wrong side

The pattern

Cast on 37 sts (an odd number — seed stitch needs it).

Border rows 1–4: Knit every stitch (garter stitch border).

Row 1 (RS): K3, *p1, k1; repeat from * to last 4 sts, k4.

Row 2 (WS): K3, *k1, p1; repeat from * to last 4 sts, k4.

(The first 3 and last 3 stitches are your garter border; the middle 31 stitches are seed stitch.)

Repeat Rows 1–2 until the piece measures approximately 7.5" from the cast-on edge, ending with a WS row.

Final border (4 rows): Knit every stitch.

Bind off loosely in knit. Weave in ends.

The one rule of seed stitch

Knit the purls, purl the knits. Each row, do the opposite of what the stitch looks like: if you see a smooth V, purl it; if you see a bump, knit it. That's the whole trick — once your hands learn it, this becomes good TV knitting.

Tips from my kitchen

  • Cotton has no stretch, so don't pull tight — keep your tension relaxed.
  • If the edges curl, widen the garter border by 2 stitches on each side.
  • Wash before first use — cotton softens up and the stitches even out.
  • Make them in sets of three in coordinating colors. They bundle beautifully as gifts.

Variations

Basketweave: work the main body in an 8-row repeat — Rows 1–4: K3, *k4, p4; repeat from * to last 3 sts, k3 (WS rows mirror: K3, *p4, k4). Rows 5–8 swap the blocks: K3, *p4, k4 on RS rows, mirrored on WS rows. Every 4 rows the blocks trade places and the woven look appears.

Striped: change colors every 8 rows for a classic look.

Scrubby: hold a strand of cotton together with a strand of textured scrubby yarn for extra exfoliating power.
